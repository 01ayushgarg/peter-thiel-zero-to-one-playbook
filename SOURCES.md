# Sources

Every quote in this repo is from one of the sources below. Before publishing, each quote was checked by script
against the raw text of its source (normalising only quote marks and whitespace), and checked again against the
specific source it cites.

## Labelling rules

- **His words** are in quotation marks with a citation. Nothing else is.
- **Class notes** are quoted with the label *from Blake Masters' class notes* (in the quote line or the
  paragraph around it). Where a class had guests, the label adds *Thiel speaking* for his lines.
- **Guests** are named whenever their view is mentioned. Their quotes are never used as Thiel's.
- **Our reading** and **The move:** mark our own interpretation and applications.
- **`[sic]`** marks a transcription error, with the likely word where it's clear (for example *one to end [sic:
  one to n]* in the NPR transcript, and *competiting [sic]* in his Reddit answer).
- **Dated numbers** carry their date: 2012 for the class notes, as of May 2014 for Google's search share, and so
  on.
- **Fictional examples** are labelled as fictional.

## Conflicts and caveats

- **Notes vs verbatim.** The CS183 notes are close to what was said but are Masters' write-up, sometimes
  reorganised into essay form. They are labelled every time. Where Thiel said the same thing later in his own
  words (the lecture, the essay, interviews), the playbook prefers his own words.
- **CS183-08 is Gibney's lecture.** Masters' notes say Bruce Gibney of Founders Fund gave it. Its questions are
  quoted once, labelled as Gibney's (chapter 11).
- **CS183-03 is second-hand.** Masters missed the class and wrote it up from two classmates' notes.
- **CS183-12's intro calls it Class 10.** It's class 12.
- **Airline revenue in 2012.** The WSJ essay gives $160 billion for the airlines [WSJ14]; the lecture gives $195
  billion in US domestic revenues [YCL5]. Both are shown in chapter 02.
- **Google's search share.** 66.4% in the 2012 notes [CS183-04], about 66% in the 2014 lecture [YCL5], and
  about 68% as of May 2014 in the essay [WSJ14].
- **The PayPal DCF.** In 2014 he says that in March 2001 about three quarters of PayPal's value came from 2011
  and beyond [YCL5]. The 2012 notes say the plan was for most value to come around 2011, and that by 2012 it
  looked like 2020 [CS183-03]. Chapter 04 shows both.
- **PayPal's early users.** The 2012 notes on class 2 mention 100 million users in 2000, which looks like an
  error. The playbook uses his own figure: "from 24 users to 1 million" between October 1999 and April 2000
  [AMA14].
- **The lean startup.** His skepticism is from the 2014 lecture Q&A [YCL5]. It's presented as his view, not a
  settled fact.
- **The YCL5 transcript** was made by Genius from the video and keeps spoken-language slips. A few garbled
  phrases were avoided rather than quoted.

## What we didn't use, and why

- **Zero to One (the book):** not in our sources. Nothing from it is quoted except lines in its published WSJ
  excerpt [WSJ14]. Its list of questions is not reproduced; chapter 11 uses the 2012 cleantech list from the
  class notes instead [CS183-14].
- ***What Happened to the Future?* (Founders Fund manifesto, c. 2011):** written by Bruce Gibney, not Thiel. Not
  used. Its tagline about flying cars and 140 characters is often attributed to Thiel; it isn't quoted here.
- ***The Education of a Libertarian* (Cato Unbound, 2009):** mostly political. Not used.
- **CS183-17 (AI) and most of CS183-15, 16 and 19:** mainly about specific technologies or guest discussion. Only
  lines on founders, distribution, recruiting and planning are used.
- **Political passages** in any source (including most of CWT24 and parts of NR11 and CWT15): out of scope.
- **Biographies, profiles and quote compilations:** secondary sources. Not used.
