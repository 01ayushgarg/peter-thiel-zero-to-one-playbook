# 00 · Who Peter Thiel is, for this playbook

This playbook is about one thing: how Peter Thiel says he thinks about startups, business strategy and
technology. It doesn't cover his politics, his political donations or his personal life.

What the sources here cover:
- **PayPal:** he co-founded it [WSJ14, NPR14]. The class notes cover its early years in detail: the 1999 switch
  to email payments, viral growth, the X.com merger, and the sale to eBay for $1.5bn [CS183-02, CS183-07,
  CS183-09, CS183-12].
- **Palantir:** he co-founded it [WSJ14, NPR14]. It started with "a focus on the intelligence community, which
  is a small submarket" [YCL5].
- **Facebook:** "Thiel was Facebook's first outside investor." [NPR14] The WSJ bio line: he "made the first
  outside investment in Facebook." [WSJ14]
- **Founders Fund:** his venture firm [NPR14, YCL5]. "The insight that foundings are crucial is what is behind
  the Founders Fund name." [CS183-06, from Blake Masters' class notes]
- **Stanford CS183 (spring 2012):** a course on startups he taught at Stanford. Student Blake Masters published
  near-verbatim notes of each class [CS183-01 to CS183-19]. The 2014 book *Zero to One*, written with Masters,
  grew out of the class [WSJ14, NPR14]. In his words: "In the class, I wanted to teach everything I knew about
  business. In the book, the goal was to pack this three-month class into a very disciplined 200 pages."
  [AMA14]
- **Stanford CS183B (fall 2014):** his guest lecture "Competition is for Losers" in Sam Altman's How to Start a
  Startup course [YCL5].

## Two kinds of source, labelled differently

- **His own words:** the 2014 lecture transcript [YCL5], his WSJ essay [WSJ14], interviews [NPR14, CWT15,
  CWT24], his Reddit answers [AMA14] and his 2011 essay [NR11].
- **Class notes:** Blake Masters' notes of the 2012 course. Masters says they are not a transcript. Every quote
  from them is labelled as from Blake Masters' class notes. Some classes had guest speakers; their views are
  named as theirs. Class 8 was given by Bruce Gibney of Founders Fund, not Thiel.

## The thesis behind everything

> "I sort of have a single idée fixe that I'm completely obsessed with on the business side which is that if
> you're starting a company, if you're the founder, entrepreneur, starting a company you always want to aim for
> monopoly and you want to always avoid competition." [YCL5]

And the wider backdrop, from his 2011 essay: "By default, computers have become the single great hope for the
technological future. The speedup in information technology contrasts dramatically with the slowdown everywhere
else." [NR11]

## Read him with his own caveats

> "If I give you some general answer, and everybody could follow it, then if everybody followed that answer, it
> would be the wrong thing to do." [CWT15]

> "If you give the core advice that you should start a business that's going to be a monopoly, and then you
> say, well that's really discouraging a lot of people who don't have an idea for a monopoly, so maybe they
> shouldn't be starting businesses." [CWT15]

> "My view is we should be starting more good businesses and fewer bad ones. Not more businesses in the
> abstract, not more start-ups in the abstract." [CWT15]

He also worries about turning ideas into formulas: "I worry that the only way you can scale things is by
somehow automating them, mechanizing them, turning them into more of a cookie cutter-type process. Then I
always worry that deranges at scale." [CWT24]

**Our reading:** this repo is a set of questions to think with, not a formula to copy. Where the honest answer is
that you maybe shouldn't start this company, the skill says so.
