# 11 · The questions

Every question he asks, in one place, each with its source. Questions in quotation marks are his wording (or the
class notes' wording, where labelled). Questions without quotation marks are our condensed version of a point he
makes, with the source next to it.

`[YCL5]`, `[WSJ14]`, `[NPR14]`, `[CWT15]`, `[AMA14]` are Thiel's own words. `[CS183-nn]` are **Blake Masters'
class notes** of the 2012 course, not a transcript.

## The cleantech list (2012)

In the 2012 class on cleantech, the notes list the areas where cleantech companies made their most important
mistakes [CS183-14]: markets, mimesis and competition, secrets, incrementalism, durability, teams,
distribution, timing, financing and luck.

> "To have a successful startup, you must have good answers—or at least a good plan for getting those
> answers—to all 10 of these points." [CS183-14, from Blake Masters' class notes]

> "And, to reiterate, you really do need all 10; 8 out of 10 is sort of a B-, and 5 of 10 earns you an F."
> [CS183-14, from Blake Masters' class notes]

In the same class, a thought experiment on thorium power scores six of the ten "right off the bat" [CS183-14].

**A note on the book.** Zero to One has its own well-known list of questions. That book isn't in our sources, so
we don't reproduce its list. The ten areas above are the 2012 class version, as written in the notes.

## A related list we don't attribute to Thiel

CS183-08 (The Pitch) was given by Bruce Gibney of Founders Fund, not Thiel. His questions include "Explain why
you are a company, not just a product/feature?" and "Why is it not likely to be displaced for some time?"
[CS183-08, Gibney's lecture, from Blake Masters' class notes]. Useful, but not Thiel's.

**Checks to run:**
1. Which of these questions can you answer in one sentence, with a number where one applies?
2. Which one did you avoid answering? That's usually the important one.
3. Score yourself on the ten cleantech areas. How many do you have a good answer or a plan for?
